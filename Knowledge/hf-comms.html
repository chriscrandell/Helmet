HF communications

High frequency (3–30 MHz): beyond-line-of-sight comms with no satellite, provided you pick the right frequency for the time of day and the distance.

How HF travels

  • Ground wave: follows the earth's surface. Useful out to tens of km over land, much further over seawater, and best at the low end of HF.
  • Skywave: refracted back down by the ionosphere. This is what gives regional to global range.
  • Skip zone: the gap between where ground wave fades and the first skywave lands. Stations there can't hear you. NVIS fills this gap.

"Higher by day, lower by night." Usable frequencies drop after sunset as ionization decays. Plan a day frequency and a night frequency, and expect the changeover around dawn and dusk.

Picking a frequency

  • MUF (maximum usable frequency): above it, the signal passes through the ionosphere and is lost.
  • LUF (lowest usable frequency): below it, D-layer absorption and noise win.
  • FOT (optimum working frequency) ≈ 85% of the MUF. Operate between LUF and FOT.
  • Use propagation prediction tools (e.g. VOACAP-based tools) for the path, date, time and sunspot activity, then confirm with ALE or a sounding.

NVIS (near-vertical incidence skywave)

  • Fires energy nearly straight up so it comes back down in a radius of roughly 0–400 km with no skip zone. Ideal for tactical and regional nets, and it works from valleys.
  • Use frequencies roughly 2–10 MHz, below the critical frequency (foF2). In practice that's the lower end at night and higher by day.
  • Antenna: a horizontal dipole mounted low (around 0.1–0.25 λ above ground), or a whip tilted back toward horizontal. A vertical whip gives poor NVIS.

ALE

Automatic link establishment (MIL-STD-188-141): radios scan a shared set of frequencies, sound them, and automatically pick the best one to link on. Operators still plan a good frequency list. ALE only chooses among the frequencies you give it.

Troubleshooting

  • Can't reach anyone: wrong frequency for the time of day or path, tuner not tuned (check VSWR), or a poor ground/counterpoise.
  • Hear them, they can't hear you: transmit power, antenna or tuner on transmit, or high noise at their end.
  • Works by day, fails at night (or the reverse): plan a frequency change.
