Troubleshooting methodology

A repeatable process beats guessing, especially at 0300 with a commander asking when it'll be back. Use the same steps every time, and work the layers.

The process

  1. Identify the problem: what exactly fails, for whom, since when, and what changed? Reproduce it yourself.
  2. Establish a theory: start with the most likely and simplest cause (cable, power, config change).
  3. Test the theory: confirm or eliminate it. If eliminated, form the next theory.
  4. Plan the fix: consider impact, get approval if it touches other users or a change window, and have a back-out plan.
  5. Implement: one change at a time.
  6. Verify: from the user's perspective, not just "the interface is up". Check you didn't break something else.
  7. Document: symptom, cause, fix and time to fix. That's the knowledge base for the next crew.

Approaches

  • Bottom-up (Layer 1 up): best when the cause is unknown or the problem is physical. Usually the default in the field.
  • Top-down (application down): when one app fails and others work.
  • Divide and conquer: start at Layer 3 (can you ping?), then go up or down based on the result. Fast for experienced techs.
  • Follow the path: trace hop by hop from source to destination.
  • Compare to known-good: swap with a working cable, port or config and diff the differences.

Common culprits

  • "Network is down" but ping works: DNS, a proxy, or the application itself.
  • Works for small packets, fails for large ones: MTU issue on a tunnel or SATCOM path.
  • Authentication fails everywhere at once: time skew (Kerberos/certs), or the domain controller isn't reachable.
  • Intermittent: duplex mismatch, a flapping link, an STP topology change, interference, or grounding.
  • Started after a change: it's probably the change. Check the change log first.
