Link budget

Will the link close, and with how much margin? Add up the gains and losses in dB from one modem to the other. The calculator below does a single downlink or uplink leg.

The equations

FSPL (dB)     = 20·log10(d_km) + 20·log10(f_GHz) + 92.45
EIRP (dBW)    = P_tx(dBW) + G_tx(dBi) − L_line(dB)
C/N0 (dB-Hz)  = EIRP − FSPL − L_other + G/T − k        k = −228.6 dBW/K/Hz
Eb/N0 (dB)    = C/N0 − 10·log10(R_bps)
Margin (dB)   = Eb/N0 − Eb/N0_required (from the modem's MODCOD table)

Dish gain (dBi) ≈ 10·log10( η · (π·D·f / c)² )   η ≈ 0.55–0.65
3 dB beamwidth (°) ≈ 70 · λ / D

dB arithmetic: everything adds and subtracts. dBW = 10·log10(watts). dBm = dBW + 30.

The defaults are illustrative only, not a real terminal or satellite. The receive G/T for an uplink is the satellite's G/T for your beam, and it comes from the operator. A full end-to-end budget combines uplink, downlink and interference C/N values. Get the GEO slant range from the look-angle calculator.

Reading the result

  • Margin < 0 dB: the link won't close. Options: reduce the data rate, use a more robust MODCOD, use a bigger dish or more power, or choose a better beam.
  • 0–3 dB: fragile. Weather or a slight mis-point drops it.
  • Typical targets: a few dB of clear-sky margin at X/C. More at Ku/Ka for rain, or rely on adaptive coding and modulation.
  • Halving the data rate buys about 3 dB. Doubling dish diameter buys about 6 dB of gain (on each end it's used).
