Power planning

Will the generator carry the package? Total up equipment draw, compare it with generator capacity, and leave headroom for start-up surges and a hot day.

The formulas

DC, or AC with PF = 1:   W  = V × A
Single-phase AC:          VA = V × A        W = V × A × PF
Three-phase AC:           VA = √3 × V(line-line) × A     W = VA × PF
kW = kVA × PF             Generators are often rated in both kW and kVA. Respect both.
  • Nameplate ratings are maximums. Real running draw is often lower. Measure with a clamp meter when you can, and keep the nameplate figure for worst-case planning.
  • Start-up surge: motors, compressors and ECUs/air conditioners can pull several times their running current for a moment. Size for the largest motor starting while everything else runs.
  • Power factor: IT loads with modern power supplies are close to 1. Motors are lower. If you don't know, 0.8 is a common planning value.

Generator loading rules of thumb

  • Plan to run at roughly 50–80% of the derated rating. That leaves room for surges and added equipment.
  • Don't run diesels lightly loaded for long periods (well under ~30%). Unburnt fuel builds up ("wet stacking"). Use load banks or right-size the generator.
  • Capacity drops with altitude and high temperature. Use the derating table in the TM.
  • Plan N+1 (a spare generator able to carry the critical load) for anything that can't go down, and a UPS to ride through the switchover.

Distribution

  • Balance the load across phases on three-phase systems. Badly unbalanced phases overheat the generator and trip breakers.
  • Keep continuous loads on a circuit at or below ~80% of the breaker rating.
  • Ground and bond per the TM and the site grounding plan. Many "network" and RF problems are actually grounding problems.
  • Label every cable and panel. The next shift has to troubleshoot it at night.
